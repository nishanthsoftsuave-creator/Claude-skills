# Agents (section lead-in)
(Source: PDF p. 535; the section's introductory warning and roadmap run onto PDF p. 536 above the "Agent Overview" heading, which belongs to C06-008)

## What the section covers
The opening of the Agents section: why intelligent agents matter, what foundation models changed, a warning that this area is experimental, and a roadmap of what the section will cover. The first lines of PDF p. 535 are the closing paragraph of the RAG part (handled in C06-006).

## Why agents matter
- Many people regard intelligent agents as the ultimate goal of AI. The book cites Russell and Norvig's classic textbook "Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach" (1995), which defines the field as "the study and design of rational agents" (PDF p. 535).
- Foundation models' unprecedented capabilities have opened the door to agentic applications once unimaginable. It now becomes possible to build autonomous, intelligent agents acting as assistants, coworkers and coaches (PDF p. 535).
- Examples of what such agents could do: create a website, gather data, plan a trip, do market research, manage a customer account, automate data entry, prepare you for interviews, interview candidates, negotiate a deal. The book says the possibilities seem endless and the potential economic value is enormous (PDF p. 535).

## Warning: an emerging field (continues on PDF p. 536)
The book warns that AI-powered agents are an emerging field with no established theoretical frameworks for defining, developing or evaluating them. The section is a best-effort framework built from existing literature and will change as the field does; it is more experimental than the rest of the book (PDF p. 536).

## Roadmap
- It begins with an overview of agents, then covers the two aspects that determine an agent's capabilities: tools and planning (PDF p. 536).
- Agents have new modes of operation and so new modes of failure; the section ends with how to evaluate agents to catch these failures (PDF p. 536).
- Although agents are novel, they build on concepts already in the book: self-critique, chain-of-thought and structured outputs (PDF p. 536).
