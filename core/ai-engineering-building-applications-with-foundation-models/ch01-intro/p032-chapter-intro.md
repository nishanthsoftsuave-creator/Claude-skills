# Chapter 1 Introduction
(Source: PDF pp. 32-33)

## What the section covers
The opening of Chapter 1: why scale defines AI since 2020, how that scale produced "model as a service" and lowered the barrier to building AI applications, why AI engineering became a fast-growing discipline, and a roadmap of what the chapter will cover.

## Meaning
- The author's one-word summary of AI after 2020 is scale. Models behind ChatGPT, Gemini and Midjourney are so large that they consume a nontrivial share of the world's electricity, and there is a risk of running out of publicly available internet data to train them (PDF p. 32).
- Model as a service: because training large language models (LLMs) demands data, compute and specialized talent that few organizations can afford, those organizations offer their models as a service for others to use (PDF p. 32).
- AI engineering is the process of building applications on top of readily available models (PDF p. 33).

## Why it matters
- Scaling up has two consequences. First, models become more powerful and capable of more tasks, which enables more applications and lets more people and teams raise productivity, create economic value and improve quality of life. Second, the cost of building models restricts it to a few players, giving rise to model as a service (PDF p. 32).
- Anyone can now build on these models without investing up front in a model of their own. Demand for AI applications has risen while the barrier to entry has fallen, which is why AI engineering has become one of the fastest-growing engineering disciplines (PDF pp. 32-33).

## Continuity with earlier ML practice
- Building applications on ML models is not new. Before LLMs, AI already powered product recommendations, fraud detection and churn prediction (PDF p. 33).
- Many principles of productionizing AI applications stay the same. What changes is that the new generation of large-scale, readily available models brings new possibilities and new challenges, which are the focus of the book (PDF p. 33).

## Chapter roadmap
1. An overview of foundation models, described as the key catalyst behind the explosion of AI engineering (PDF p. 33).
2. A range of successful AI use cases, each showing what AI is and is not yet good at. Because capabilities expand daily, future possibilities are hard to predict, but existing application patterns help reveal opportunities now and hint at future use (PDF p. 33).
3. An overview of the new AI stack: what changed with foundation models, what stayed the same, and how an AI engineer's role differs from that of a traditional ML engineer (PDF p. 33).

## Time-sensitive statements
- The remarks about electricity consumption and the risk of exhausting public internet data reflect the state of the field at the time of writing (PDF p. 32).
