# Planning AI Applications (lead-in)
(Source: PDF p. 78)

## What the section covers
The short introduction to the "Planning AI Applications" section, which begins midway down PDF p. 78 after the workflow-automation discussion (C01-013).

## Meaning and framing
- Because AI's potential looks almost limitless, it is tempting to jump straight into building applications (PDF p. 78).
- If the goal is only to learn and have fun, the author says to jump right in, since building is one of the best ways to learn. In the early days of foundation models, several heads of AI told the author they encouraged their teams to experiment with AI applications in order to upskill (PDF p. 78).

## When and how to apply
If building is a job rather than a hobby, it is worth stepping back to ask why you are building the application and how to go about it (PDF p. 78).

## Why it matters
The central contrast of the lead-in: it is easy to build a cool demo with foundation models, but hard to create a profitable product (PDF p. 78). INTERPRETATION: this contrast motivates the structured planning steps that follow in the book (use case evaluation, setting expectations, milestone planning); PDF p. 78 itself does not list them.

## Relations
The first planning step is use case evaluation (C01-015); later steps are setting expectations (C01-016).
