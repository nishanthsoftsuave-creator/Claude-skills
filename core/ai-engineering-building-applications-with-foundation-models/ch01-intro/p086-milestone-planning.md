# Milestone Planning
(Source: PDF pp. 86-87)

## What the section covers
How to turn measurable goals into a plan: start from an evaluation of existing models, expect the goals to shift after that evaluation, and budget for the "last mile" because a quick demo says little about how long a real product takes. (The usefulness-threshold metrics list that sits at the top of PDF p. 86 belongs to the preceding Setting Expectations row, C01-016.)

## How it works
- Once measurable goals exist, a plan is needed to reach them, and the route depends on the starting point (PDF p. 86).
- The first planning step is to evaluate existing models to learn what they can already do. The stronger the off-the-shelf model, the less work remains for the team (PDF p. 86).
- Worked example: with a goal of automating 60% of customer support tickets, a model that already handles 30% of tickets means less effort than one that handles none (PDF pp. 86-87).

## Goals can change after evaluation
Evaluation may reveal that the resources needed to bring the app up to its usefulness threshold exceed its potential return. In that case the team may decide not to pursue it at all (PDF p. 87).

## The last mile challenge
- Early success with foundation models can mislead. Their base capabilities are impressive, so a fun demo may take little time, yet a good demo does not promise a good product. A demo might take a weekend; a product can take months or even years (PDF p. 87).
- Ding et al. (2023), in the UltraChat paper, said the journey "from 0 to 60 is easy" while going from 60 to 100 becomes "exceedingly challenging" (PDF p. 87).
- LinkedIn (2024) reported the same pattern. Reaching 80% of the desired experience took one month, and that early progress led them to badly underestimate the remaining effort. Getting beyond 95% took four more months. Much of that time went to product kinks and hallucinations, and the slow pace of each extra 1% gain was discouraging (PDF p. 87).

## When and how to apply
INTERPRETATION: when scheduling an AI product, baseline the candidate models first, size the gap between baseline and threshold, and do not extrapolate demo speed to production timelines; budget the long tail separately.
