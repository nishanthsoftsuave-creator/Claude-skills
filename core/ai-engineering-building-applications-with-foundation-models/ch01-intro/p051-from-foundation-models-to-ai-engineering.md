# From Foundation Models to AI Engineering
(Source: PDF pp. 51-56)

## What the section covers
Why AI engineering has become a distinct and fast-growing discipline. The author defines it, contrasts it with older ML engineering, names three factors behind its rise (general-purpose capabilities, rising investment, a low barrier to entry), gives growth evidence, and closes with a sidebar on why the book uses the term "AI engineering".

## Meaning
- AI engineering is the process of building applications on top of foundation models (PDF p. 51).
- People have built AI applications for more than ten years, a practice usually called ML engineering or MLOps (ML operations) (PDF p. 51).
- The core contrast: traditional ML engineering develops ML models, whereas AI engineering leverages models that already exist (PDF p. 51).

## Why it matters: three factors behind the growth
The book says the availability and accessibility of strong foundation models create three conditions that together drive rapid growth (PDF p. 51).

### Factor 1: general-purpose AI capabilities
- Foundation models are powerful not only because they do existing tasks better but because they can do more tasks. Things once thought impossible became possible, new kinds of applications keep appearing, and applications that look impossible today may work tomorrow. This widens the user base and the demand for AI applications (PDF p. 51).
- Example reasoning: since AI can write about as well as people, sometimes better, it can fully or partly automate any task that involves communication, "which is pretty much everything" (PDF p. 51). Cited uses: writing emails, answering customer requests, explaining complex contracts (PDF pp. 51-52).
- Anyone with a computer can generate customized, high-quality images and video for marketing material, professional headshots, art concepts and book illustration (PDF p. 52).
- AI can also synthesize training data, develop algorithms and write code, which helps train still more powerful models later (PDF p. 52).

### Factor 2: increased AI investment
- The success of ChatGPT sharply increased investment from venture capital and from enterprises. As AI applications get cheaper and quicker to bring to market, the return on investment looks better, and companies rush to put AI in products and processes (PDF p. 52).
- Data point: Matt Ross, a senior manager of applied research at Scribd, said the estimated AI cost for his use cases fell by two orders of magnitude between April 2022 and April 2023 (PDF p. 52).
- Goldman Sachs Research estimated AI investment could approach $100 billion in the US and $200 billion worldwide by 2025 (PDF p. 52).
- AI is often named as a competitive advantage. FactSet found one in three S&P 500 companies mentioned AI on their second-quarter 2023 earnings calls, three times the number a year before (PDF p. 52).
- Figure 1-5 (rendered and read): a line chart of S&P 500 companies citing AI per quarter. The count hovers roughly between 40 and 70 from Q3 2018 through Q3 2022 (for example 46 in Q3/18, 70 in Q1/21, 53 in Q3/22), then jumps to 78 (Q4/22), 117 (Q1/23) and 177 (Q2/23), a record (PDF p. 53).
- WallStreetZen reportedly found that companies mentioning AI on earnings calls saw an average stock rise of 4.6% versus 2.4% for those that did not. The book flags that it is unclear whether this is causation (AI makes firms more successful) or correlation (successful firms adopt new technology quickly) (PDF p. 53).

### Factor 3: low entrance barrier
- The "model as a service" approach, popularized by OpenAI and other providers, exposes models through APIs that take user queries and return outputs. Without such APIs you would need your own infrastructure to host and serve a model; with them, a single API call reaches a powerful model (PDF pp. 53-54).
- AI also enables building with minimal coding: it can write code for people without software backgrounds so they can quickly put ideas in front of users, and you can work with models in plain English rather than a programming language. The author's phrasing: anyone can now develop AI applications (PDF p. 54).
- Developing foundation models themselves is feasible only for big corporations (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Baidu, Tencent), governments (Japan, the UAE) and ambitious, well-funded startups (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral) because of the resources required (PDF p. 54).
- In a September 2022 interview Sam Altman (OpenAI CEO) said the biggest opportunity for the vast majority of people will be adapting these models for specific applications (PDF p. 54).

## Evidence of rapid growth
- The book calls AI engineering one of the fastest, and quite possibly the fastest, growing engineering disciplines, with tooling gaining traction faster than any earlier software engineering tools (PDF p. 54).
- Within two years, four open source AI engineering tools (AutoGPT, Stable Diffusion web UI, LangChain, Ollama) gathered more GitHub stars than Bitcoin, and were on track to pass popular web frameworks such as React and Vue (PDF p. 54).
- Figure 1-6 (rendered and read): a star-history line chart. React and Vue climb steadily from about 2013-2014 to roughly 215-230K stars by 2024; Bitcoin reaches somewhat under 100K; AutoGPT (about 175K) and Stable Diffusion web UI (about 145K) shoot up steeply from 2022-2023, while LangChain and Ollama reach about 100K by 2024 (PDF p. 55). Values are read approximately off the chart.
- A LinkedIn survey from August 2023: professionals adding terms such as "Generative AI", "ChatGPT", "Prompt Engineering" and "Prompt Crafting" to their profiles grew on average 75% per month. ComputerWorld declared that teaching AI to behave is the fastest-growing career skill (PDF p. 55).

## Sidebar: why the term "AI engineering"
- Many labels exist for building on foundation models: ML engineering, MLOps, AIOps, LLMOps and others (PDF p. 56).
- "ML engineering" was rejected because working with foundation models differs from working with traditional ML models in several important ways (discussed in the section on AI engineering versus ML engineering), and the term would not capture that difference. The author still sees ML engineering as a good umbrella covering both processes (PDF p. 56).
- The "Ops" terms were rejected because, although the work has operational parts, the focus is more on tweaking (engineering) foundation models to do what you want (PDF p. 56).
- Finally the author surveyed 20 people building on foundation models; most preferred "AI engineering", and the author followed them (PDF p. 56).
- The section ends by noting the community's creativity and previewing the next section on common application patterns (PDF p. 56).
