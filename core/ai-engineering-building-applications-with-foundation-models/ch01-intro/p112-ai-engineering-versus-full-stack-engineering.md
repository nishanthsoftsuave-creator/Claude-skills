# AI Engineering Versus Full-Stack Engineering
(Source: PDF p. 112, tail page 113)

## What the section covers
Why the growing weight of application development, especially interfaces, pulls AI engineering toward full-stack development, and how the workflow order changes.

## Table 1-6 (belongs to the preceding section, C01-021, but is printed here)
The table is announced at the end of C01-021's last page and printed at the top of this page, above this section's heading, so it is recorded here. It compares app-development categories, traditional ML versus foundation models (PDF p. 112): AI interface goes from less important to important; prompt engineering goes from not applicable to important; evaluation goes from important to more important.

## Meaning and why it matters
- Greater emphasis on application development, especially interfaces, brings AI engineering closer to full-stack development, and the importance of interfaces is shifting AI tooling to attract more frontend engineers (PDF p. 112).
- Classic ML engineering is Python-centric, and before foundation models the popular frameworks mostly offered Python APIs. Python remains popular, but JavaScript support is growing: LangChain.js, Transformers.js, OpenAI's Node library and Vercel's AI SDK (PDF p. 112).
- Many AI engineers still come from ML backgrounds, but more arrive from web development or full-stack backgrounds (PDF p. 112).

## Full-stack advantage and the changed workflow
- Full-stack engineers can quickly turn ideas into demos, collect feedback and iterate (PDF p. 113).
- Traditional ML starts with gathering data and training a model, and builds the product last. With models readily available, one can build the product first and invest in data and models only once it shows promise (PDF p. 113). Figure 1-16 shows two rows: ML Engineering as Data, then Model, then Product; AI Engineering as Product, then Data, then Model. Its caption says the new workflow rewards fast iteration (PDF p. 113).
- In traditional ML, model and product development are often disjointed and ML engineers are rarely part of product decisions; with foundation models, AI engineers are much more involved in building the product (PDF p. 113).
