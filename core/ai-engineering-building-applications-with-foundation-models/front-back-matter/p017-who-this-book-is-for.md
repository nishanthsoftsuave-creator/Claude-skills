# Preface - Who This Book Is For
(Source: PDF pp. 17-18, tail PDF p. 19. The top of PDF p. 17 finishes the prerequisites list of the previous section, covered in P-003. The heading starts on PDF p. 18. The "Navigating This Book" heading on PDF p. 19 belongs to P-005.)

## What the section covers
The intended readership, the scenarios in which the book applies, secondary audiences, and a note on technical depth.

## Primary audience
- Anyone who wants to use foundation models to solve real-world problems. It is a technical book, so the language targets technical roles: AI engineers, ML engineers, data scientists, engineering managers, and technical product managers (PDF p. 18).
- It fits readers who recognize one of these scenarios (PDF p. 18):
  - You are building or optimizing an AI application, from scratch or moving beyond a demo toward production, possibly facing hallucinations, security, latency, or cost issues and needing targeted solutions.
  - You want your team's AI development process to be more systematic, faster, and more reliable.
  - You want to understand how your organization can use foundation models to improve the bottom line, and how to build a team for it.

## Secondary audiences
The book can also benefit (PDF pp. 18-19):
- Tool developers who want to spot underserved areas in AI engineering to position products in the ecosystem.
- Researchers who want a better grasp of AI use cases.
- Job candidates who want clarity about skills needed for an AI engineering career (PDF p. 19).
- Anyone wanting to understand AI's capabilities and limitations and how it could affect different roles (PDF p. 19).

## Depth warning
The author likes getting to the bottom of things, so some sections go deeper technically. Early readers often liked the detail, though it may not suit everyone. The author promises a heads-up before sections become very technical, and readers may skip ahead if it gets too in the weeds (PDF p. 19).
