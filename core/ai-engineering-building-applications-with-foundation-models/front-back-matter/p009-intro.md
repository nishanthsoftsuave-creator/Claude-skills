# Preface (Opening)
(Source: PDF pp. 9-10; the closing lines of the opening, about the author's learning process and feedback channels, sit on PDF p. 11 above the "What This Book Is About" heading)

## What the section covers
The author's personal opening to the book: why ChatGPT's arrival was disorienting, why the explosion of applications (not the model's size) was the real surprise, how AI engineering rests on long-standing techniques while still raising new problems, and how the book was researched.

## Meaning
- Scaling a model up to improve it was already known in the AI community for over a decade. The author cites the AlexNet authors' 2012 remark that their results could be improved "simply by waiting for faster GPUs and bigger datasets" (PDF p. 9).
- The surprise was not model size or capability but how many applications the capability boost unlocked. The author had expected a small gain in quality metrics to yield a modest rise in applications; the result was an explosion of new possibilities (PDF p. 9).
- The new capabilities raised demand for AI applications and also lowered the barrier for developers. Starting is easy, and one can even build an application without writing any code. AI thereby shifted from a specialized discipline into a development tool anyone can use (PDF p. 9).

## Why it matters
- The framing sets the book's thesis: AI engineering is both continuous with earlier practice and genuinely new. Adoption looks new, but it is built on old techniques: language-modeling papers date to the 1950s, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) rests on retrieval technology that powered search and recommender systems long before the name RAG existed (PDF pp. 9-10).
- The best practices for deploying traditional ML applications are said to remain the best practices for foundation-model applications: systematic experimentation, rigorous evaluation, and relentless optimization for faster and cheaper models (PDF p. 10).
- Familiarity and ease of use can mislead people into believing there is nothing new. The author's counterpoint: many principles stay the same, but the scale and improved capability of the models bring opportunities and challenges that need new solutions (PDF p. 10).

## Scope statement
The book covers the end-to-end process of adapting foundation models to real-world problems, combining proven techniques from other engineering fields with techniques emerging specifically with foundation models (PDF p. 10).

## How the book was researched
- The author wrote to learn, drawing on projects worked on, papers read, and people interviewed (PDF p. 10).
- Notes came from over 100 conversations and interviews. Interviewees included researchers at major AI labs (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and others), framework developers (NVIDIA, Meta, Hugging Face, Anyscale, LangChain, LlamaIndex and others), executives and heads of AI or data at companies of varied size, product managers, community researchers, and independent application developers (PDF p. 10).
- Early readers tested assumptions and brought new perspectives; some sections drew thousands of community comments after being posted on the author's blog, giving new views or confirming hypotheses (PDF p. 11).
- The author invites continued feedback via X, LinkedIn, or email, with the address given in the book (PDF p. 11).
