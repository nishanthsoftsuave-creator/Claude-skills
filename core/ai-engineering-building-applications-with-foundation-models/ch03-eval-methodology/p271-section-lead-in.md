# AI as a Judge (Lead-in)
(Source: PDF p. 271)

## What the section covers
The opening of the AI as a judge section: the motivation, the definition of the terms, when the approach became practical, and evidence of how widespread it is. (The top of this page finishes the embedding discussion, covered in C03-012.)

## Meaning
- Evaluating open-ended responses is hard, and the difficulty has pushed many teams back to human evaluation. Since AI has automated many hard tasks, the question is whether it can automate evaluation too (PDF p. 271).
- Using AI to evaluate AI is called AI as a judge or LLM as a judge. A model used to evaluate other models is an AI judge (PDF p. 271).

## Why it matters
- The idea of automating evaluation with AI is old, but it only became practical once models were capable enough, around 2020 with the release of GPT-3 (PDF p. 271).
- As of the book's writing, AI as a judge is one of the most common, perhaps the most common, ways to evaluate AI models in production. The author observed that most AI-evaluation startup demos in 2023 and 2024 used it in some form (PDF p. 271).
- The page ends mid-sentence: LangChain's State of AI report in 2023 "noted that 58% of" (PDF p. 271). The rest of the statement (what the 58% refers to) is on the next page and is recorded in C03-014. That the approach is an active research area is also stated on the next page (C03-014).
