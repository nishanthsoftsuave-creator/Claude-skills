# Introduction to Hacking Web Applications - The Hacker's Mindset
(Source: PDF p. 145; the discussion finishes at the top of PDF p. 146)

## What the section covers
The opening of chapter 9 (printed above its first heading and described here) and the mental habits that make a good hacker.

## Chapter opening
- This part builds on recon to teach concrete exploits for web application vulnerabilities and puts the reader in the role of a hacker (PDF p. 145).
- The hypothetical target throughout is mega-bank.com, introduced in Part I. The exploits are extremely common and can be carried to other applications as long as Part I's recon is used too (PDF p. 145).
- By the end the reader should have the recon skill to find exploitable bugs and the offensive skill to build and deploy payloads against them (PDF p. 145).

## Meaning: the mindset
- Success needs a particular mindset as well as skills (PDF p. 145).
- Software engineers measure productivity by features added or improvements made, such as a 10% performance gain; this is measurable even if less so than in traditional jobs (PDF p. 145).
- Hackers measure productivity in ways that are far harder to see, because most hacking is gathering and analysing data (PDF p. 145). That work is full of false positives and can look like wasted time to an onlooker (PDF p. 146).

## How it works
- Most hackers do not take software apart or alter it; they study it to work with the existing code, looking for entrypoints instead of making them. The skills are often like those in Part I (PDF p. 146).
- Any codebase holds exploitable bugs, so a good hacker looks constantly for clues (PDF p. 146).
- Even a good hacker may wait a long while for a result; weeks or months of analysis may pass before an entrypoint is found and an exploit built (PDF p. 146).

## When and how to apply
- Keep pushing toward finding and delivering a payload. Record earlier attempts and lessons carefully; the detail pays off as you move to larger applications, especially ones with key functionality or data as the target (PDF p. 146).
- Hackers must keep improving their skills or be outdone by defenders, as the history chapter showed. Old methods lose value as the web changes (PDF p. 146).
- The book sums up: a hacker is first a detective; a good one is organised; a great one adds technical depth; a master keeps learning as defenders improve (PDF p. 146).
