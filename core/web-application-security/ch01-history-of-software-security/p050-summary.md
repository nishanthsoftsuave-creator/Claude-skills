# Chapter 1 Summary - The History of Software Security
(Source: PDF p. 50)

## What the section covers
The chapter's closing argument: security is a cycle in which defenders and attackers keep responding to each other, and the most valuable skill is independent problem solving.

## Main points
- The origins of software security, and of hackers who try to bypass it, reach back at least around a hundred years. Today's software and its security build on lessons from older technology (PDF p. 50).
- Hackers once targeted applications differently. As one part of the application stack becomes more secure, hackers move to newly emerging technologies, which often lack the same built-in controls. Only through trial and error do engineers learn to design and apply the right controls (PDF p. 50).
- Simple early websites were riddled with holes, especially at the server and network level. Modern web applications bring new surface area for attackers, and it is being actively exploited (PDF p. 50).
- The history shows that today's concerns are one stage of a cyclical process. Future web applications will be more secure, and hackers will probably move to a new attack surface, with RTC or web sockets given as possible examples (PDF p. 50).

## Practical tip
A highlighted tip says each new technology brings its own attack surface and vulnerabilities. One way to become an excellent hacker is to follow new technologies closely, since they often hold security holes that have not been published or found (PDF p. 50).

## What the book promises
The book will show how to break into and secure modern web applications, but the author says modern offensive and defensive techniques are only one part of what to take away. The most valuable skill for a security professional is being able to find your own solutions to security problems. Readers who gain the critical thinking and problem solving skills from the coming chapters will stand out when unusual exploits appear or unfamiliar defences get in their way (PDF p. 50).
