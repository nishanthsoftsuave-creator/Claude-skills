# The Origins of Hacking
(Source: PDF p. 33, with the closing paragraphs on PDF p. 34)

## What the section covers
The chapter opening: why a historical overview belongs in a security book, and where the author places the beginning of hacking.

## Why history first
Before offensive and defensive techniques, the reader needs some understanding of software security's long history. A brief overview of major security events over roughly the last hundred years is meant to explain the technology underneath today's web applications. It also shows the ongoing relationship between developing security mechanisms and forward-thinking hackers who improvise ways to break or bypass them (PDF p. 33).

## Where hacking began
- Hackers have attracted more publicity in the last two decades than ever, so someone without background may assume hacking is tied to the internet and that most hackers appeared in the past 20 years (PDF p. 33).
- The author calls that only a partial truth. The number of hackers exploded with the rise of the World Wide Web, but hackers have existed since the middle of the 20th century, possibly earlier depending on what counts as hacking (PDF p. 33).
- Experts debate the decade of the true origin because a few events in the early 1900s closely resemble hacking today. Isolated incidents in the 1910s and 1920s most likely qualify, mostly tampering with Morse code senders and receivers or interfering with radio wave transmission. They were uncommon, and it is hard to point to large-scale operations disrupted by abuse of those technologies (PDF p. 33).

## Author's framing and scope
- The author says that they are not a historian but a security professional who finds solutions to deep architectural and code-level security issues in enterprise software, after years as a software engineer writing web applications in many languages and frameworks, and who still writes security automation and hobby projects (PDF p. 34).
- The chapter therefore is not meant to argue specifics or alternative origin stories. It is compiled from years of independent research, with emphasis on lessons that can be applied today (PDF p. 34).
- Because it is a reference for critical events and not a comprehensive history, the timeline starts in the early 1930s (PDF p. 34).
