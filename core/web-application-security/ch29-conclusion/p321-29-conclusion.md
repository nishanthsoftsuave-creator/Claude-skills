# Conclusion
(Source: PDF pp. 321-322)

## What the section covers
The book's short closing chapter. It tells the reader the book is finished, says what the book is not, gives the three criteria used to choose its topics, and ends with the writer's wish for the reader.

## Meaning
- The reader is told they have finished the book and have, ideally, learned a lot about securing and exploiting web applications that can be applied elsewhere (PDF p. 321).
- The book is not presented as a complete glossary of web application security lessons. Becoming an expert in the field will mean meeting many more topics, technologies and scenarios (PDF p. 321).

## How the topics were chosen
The book says three criteria guided the topic list (PDF p. 321):
1. Each topic had to apply to a wide range of web applications, so that the information is practical and can be put to use after reading.
2. Each topic had to sit at the recommended skill level, or at a level the reader could reach from earlier chapters. Difficulty and required knowledge therefore had to scale linearly with what came before. The book could not skip around and expect readers to find the missing knowledge elsewhere, because that would turn it into a glossary-style reference instead of something to read straight through.
3. Each topic had to be related to the others, so the book flows from cover to cover. In the writer's own reading, few technical books and fewer security books were organized well enough to pick up and continue learning where one stopped, without jumping back and forth or turning to a search engine.

## Limitations the book admits
The writer cannot promise that the book meets all three goals, only that the contents were organized and curated as well as possible, in the hope that readers learn a lot and enjoy it (PDF p. 321).

## Closing wish
The writer says the last year of writing the book was very enjoyable, and that they would be overwhelmed with happiness if the contents helped someone become a better engineer, fix a security flaw in an application, or get a job in the security industry, and ends by thanking the reader and wishing them well in future security work (PDF p. 322).
